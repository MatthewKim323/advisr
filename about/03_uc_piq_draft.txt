PIQ #2: Every person has a creative side, and it can be expressed in many ways: problem
solving, original and innovative thinking, and artistically, to name a few. Describe how
you express your creative side.
Word count: 343 / 350

My creativity lives in the space between what something is and what it could become.

My grandmother, Lola Marites, keeps a collection of buttons in an old biscuit tin. They
are buttons from blouses that fell apart decades ago, buttons from uniforms that no longer
have owners, buttons with histories she can still recite. When I was young, she let me
arrange them. I would sort them by color, then by size, then by the way they caught the
light. She never told me the "right" way to sort them. She trusted me to find my own
order.

I think this is where my creative process began — in the quiet discipline of finding new
patterns in familiar things. It is the same impulse that drew me to computer science,
which I have come to love precisely because it rewards this kind of attention. A piece of
code is like my grandmother's buttons: it is a collection of small, repeated elements that
can be arranged, infinitely, into something new.

Last year, my robotics team was working on an arm attachment for a wheelchair. I was
responsible for the gripping mechanism. Everyone else wanted to make it stronger, faster,
more industrial. I asked a different question: how do you hold something the way a human
holds it — gently, adaptively, without breaking it? The answer came from an unexpected
place: silicone molds from baking supply stores. We iterated through six designs. The
final version could grip an eggshell without cracking it.

I believe creativity is often just the willingness to take seriously the problems other
people dismiss. It is learning to see buttons as a sorting problem, silicone as an
engineering material, and patience, like my grandmother taught me, as a design principle.

That is how I create.
