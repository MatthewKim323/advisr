Common App Prompt 1 — Draft 2

My mother keeps every grocery receipt in a shoebox under her bed. Not for
taxes — just so she knows, when the week ends, how much we actually spent
on eggs and on everything else. She grew up in a house where money had to
prove its trail, and she brought that habit with her into ours.

For a long time I thought of it as her weird thing. Then I got my first
camera — a Nikon D40 my aunt gave me when she upgraded — and I started
noticing what my mother noticed. The worn corner of the cereal box. The
way my brother put his sneakers on the couch without thinking. Stuff I had
walked past for years suddenly had a shape to it.

My photography teacher, Ms. Holloway, told me at the end of sophomore year
that I had "the eye that gets you past the front door at any art school."
She said it like it was nothing and I think about it every day.

This summer I shot a series for the Waycross Historical Society —
interior photos of our AME church, Bethlehem AME, before they finished
the renovation. The deacons let me in at seven in the morning, before
anyone else was there. I took 340 frames. I kept 11. I'm not interested
in being a photographer who takes pretty pictures; I'm interested in the
question my mother asks every Sunday night over the shoebox, which is
what were we actually doing with this week.

I want to study photography and art history. I want to make work that
remembers the South the way my mother remembers groceries. And one day I
want to show my own children why the front porch at 4am is the most
beautiful hour I know.
